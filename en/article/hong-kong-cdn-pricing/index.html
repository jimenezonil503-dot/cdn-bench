Hong Kong CDN Pricing: Published Plans and Network Cost Differences

Understand published Hong Kong-related plans and the delivery, request, security and origin charges needed for a comparable budget.

A price needs a workload definition

Hong Kong delivery can combine subscriptions, transfer, bandwidth, requests, security and origin charges. Define ordinary/peak traffic, request volume, dynamic share, origin region and carriers before requesting a quote.

Prices were checked on 14 October 2026, in USD, without assuming tax or fixed currency conversion. Global bundles are not dedicated Hong Kong quotes. See routing and protected-service costs.

A low subscription does not ensure a low bill

Google pricing separates delivery, fill and lookups; Azure pricing combines base, regional transfer and requests. Compare them with bundles using identical workloads and security/support scope.

Model base, delivery, origin, request/security, logging and support costs plus migration. Ask how blocked traffic, scrubbing and overages are billed. Unknown charges should remain unresolved rather than assumed zero.

Traffic and bandwidth models react differently to bursts

Byte-based billing tracks transferred data; bandwidth contracts depend on peaks, sampling and the specified statistic. Monthly average bandwidth cannot validate peak commitments. Short concentrated downloads can raise capacity needs disproportionately.

Model ordinary, launch and abnormal-load days as budget scenarios, not benchmarks. Treat compression/cache savings as variables. Confirm video and large-file eligibility instead of assuming a web bundle covers them.

Match annual prepayment to the expected term

At published CDN5 Standard prices, twelve monthly payments total $5,988 versus $5,000 annual prepayment, before tax, extras or future changes. Evaluate expected project duration, refund/downgrade terms and capital commitment. Seasonal workloads may value flexibility more than a lower nominal rate.

A route premium should buy a verifiable benefit

Pay route premiums only for observed benefits to the main audience: fewer failures, better tails and completed tasks. Mainland-oriented transit may offer little value to a predominantly Southeast Asian audience.

Quotes should specify route scope, mitigation behavior, change notices and support. CN2 is not a universal bidirectional/carrier guarantee. Pilot CDN5 where relevant and verify actual resources; ask global products about regional controls and their cost.

Make quote and invoice checks repeatable

Agree on units, timezone, currency, tax, commitments, credits and renewal. Annual prepayment can reduce monthly equivalents while increasing exit constraints; show the payment term explicitly.

Reconcile exports against invoices, including regional attribution, fill, failures and retries. Assign budget-alert ownership so cost increases are explained while they occur.

Preserve dates, plan versions and source links. Rerun the same workload model at renewal or scope changes to distinguish price movements from workload changes.
