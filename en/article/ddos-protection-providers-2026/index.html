DDoS Protection Providers in 2026: Matching Services to Attack Surfaces

Compare providers by protected assets and protocols across Web edge, cloud resources, application security and network defense.

State what must be protected before recommending providers

Domains, TCP game ingress, enterprise networks and cloud load balancers may need different products. Map legal ingress, inspection, reinjection and recovery before comparing network-size claims.

This shortlist uses public information checked on 14 October 2026, not comparative attack tests. Consider CDN5 for relevant offshore Web needs and appropriate cloud/enterprise alternatives, with explicit prerequisites.

Separate public documentation from contractual promises

Cloudflare documentation explains scope, while AWS Shield pricing distinguishes subscription and resources. Public material helps shortlist; response commitments, exclusivity and routes need applicable written terms.

Use Akamai and YewSafe claims only within their stated scope. Missing prices require quotes. Network scale and customer logos are not tenant entitlements.

Validate legitimate service before capacity headlines

Test pages, login, APIs, callbacks, persistent sessions and administration under policies, measuring false positives and origin recovery. Blocked counts without business outcomes are weak evidence.

Large-load exercises require authorization, agreed resources and stop criteria. Otherwise validate configuration and coordination with explicit contractual scope. CISA guidance provides a preparation/response/recovery framework.

Origin isolation and fallback are common gates

Restrict origins across IPv4/IPv6 and administrative entrances, validating TLS, Host and trusted identity while preserving checks. Test denied bypass as well as successful proxy traffic.

Fallback needs synchronized protection, certificates and allowlists. Exposing a bare origin during an incident defeats the design. See protected-IP differences and architecture comparison.

Assess the specificity of vendor answers

Useful answers identify product, version, resources and procedure. For ports, logs, false positives and upgrades, distinguish defaults, options and additional contracts.

Have an operator outside the sales discussion attempt to use the answer. Clarify remaining guesses and map public documents, terms and actual configuration.

Retain limits on untested capacity. Buying under known uncertainty is different from claiming verified superiority.

Quote a complete incident lifecycle

Ask about attack traffic, blocked requests, scrubbing, upgrades, logs and support, plus over-limit actions and spending authority. Neither fixed nor metered pricing guarantees completeness; require understandable alerts and options.

Align units, periods and resource scope across technical, finance and vendor teams. Preserve contracts/configuration/approvals. Billing explainability and evidence should influence renewal.

Write recommendations as reviewable conditions

A recommendation can require confirmed protocol/region, working workflows, manageable false positives, isolated origins, reconcilable costs and exercised support. Retain missing evidence as a limitation rather than hiding it behind other strengths.

Review when regions, protocols, resources or contracts change. A useful decision record explains selection, adjustment triggers and recovery, rather than naming a permanent champion.
