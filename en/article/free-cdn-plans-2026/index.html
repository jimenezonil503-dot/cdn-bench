Pricing and procurement

Free CDN Options in 2026: Service Limits and Performance

Distinguish free plans, recurring allowances and trial credits. Compare public Cloudflare, CloudFront, Fastly and CDN5 conditions for low-risk publishing and evaluation.

CDN Bench editorial · 2026-10-14 · Updated 2026-10-147 min read

  6. Upgrade for a concrete missing capability

Identify what is actually free

Blogs, documentation and low-risk public assets can start with a free CDN if usage, configuration and support limitations fit. A free product tier, recurring metered allowance and expiring trial credit are different commitments.

Conditions were checked on 14 October 2026 and should be rechecked at signup. Opening a page for free is not complete business assurance, but small publishers do not automatically need the highest tier. Use the CDN primer to identify the required paths.

Keep domain ownership, backups and portable configuration even on a free service. A zero subscription does not remove the need for operational records or an exit plan.

Four public entry points with different limits

Cloudflare, CloudFront, Fastly and CDN5 offer different entry points. Verify the full product conditions for logs, rules, support and permitted use.

A free allowance does not define the whole bill

Storage, load balancers, origin servers and logs can have separate charges. Check payment requirements, reset periods, over-limit behavior and cancellation before activation, with budget alerts.

Do not rely on account splitting to evade terms. Measure object size, compression, caching and abnormal traffic. Separate allowances from total use and retain the applicable overage or suspension rule.

Free-tier performance needs workload evidence

Price alone does not establish regional performance. Compare identical content and origins across hits, misses, dynamic paths, carriers and peaks, including failures and correctness. Revalidate after moving to a different paid product.

Browser and origin observations can partly compensate for limited edge logs, but the gap remains. It may be acceptable for low-risk documentation and unacceptable for incident-sensitive services. Make that trade-off explicitly.

Free deployment still requires safe caching

Apply RFC 9111 semantics regardless of price. Limited rule counts are a reason for clear scope, not unsafe global caching. Respect identity, language, size and version dimensions.

Test separate accounts, logout and updates. Contain any cross-user exposure immediately. Origin TLS, ingress restrictions and administrative controls remain deployment responsibilities even on free tiers.

Upgrade for a concrete missing capability

Upgrade for identifiable needs: support commitments, rules, logs, persistent sessions, regions or allowances. A database bottleneck may not benefit, and media may require a different product. CDN5’s free test and paid acceleration capabilities differ materially.

Preserve configuration and baseline before upgrading; confirm scheduling, billing and policy changes plus downgrade terms. Use the buying guide to connect spending to measurable needs.

Keep an exit path even when the plan is free

Plans and terms can change. Keep domain control, exports, certificate procedures and necessary logs. Migrate through protected entrances and revoke obsolete tokens and origin permissions, checking related charges.

Reassess as workloads gain membership, payments or real-time functions. A free CDN can reduce startup cost within clear limits; a zero subscription is not evidence of unlimited capability or assurance.

Assign ownership for plan-change notices and maintain reachable account and domain contacts.
