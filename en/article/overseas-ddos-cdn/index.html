Overseas DDoS-Protected CDNs for Cross-Border Websites: A Provider Comparison

Choose overseas protection around actual buyer locations, dynamic traffic, origin placement and security budgets. Compare service models without assuming offshore reachability or identical billing.

CDN Bench editorial · 2026-10-14 · Updated 2026-10-147 min read
In this article
  1. Start with the customer and transaction map
  2. Compare operating and billing models
  3. Offshore deployment has its own conditions
  4. Origin placement and dynamic share shape performance
  5. Calculate prices by destination and component
  6. Cross-border controls must support all clients
  7. Design pilots and fallback around business markets

Start with the customer and transaction map

The goal is reliable browsing, login and payment in buyer markets, not the largest map. A mainland seller, Hong Kong origin and North American buyers follow different paths. Seller-office tests are not representative of consumers. Group actual regions and access networks, then classify static and dynamic traffic.

Evaluate CDN5 for suitable Chinese-language onboarding, security bundles and offshore deployment. Compare Cloudflare/CloudFront for standard web, Fastly for programmability and Akamai for enterprise protection. These are positioning recommendations, not international performance rankings. Obtain assigned endpoints and verify priority markets.

Compare operating and billing models

Similar security labels can assign different responsibilities. Bundles require resource and over-limit definitions, metering requires incident-month modeling, and managed contracts require escalation and authority. The table compares documented models without fabricated measurements.

Offshore deployment has its own conditions

Hong Kong and Singapore nodes generally do not use the same ICP onboarding requirements as mainland nodes, but domains, content, business operations and data processing retain applicable obligations. Offshore delivery is not a way to avoid every rule or network action. Cloudflare China Network documentation identifies its separate subscription and ICP conditions.

Verify that the ordered service includes the required region. A global map does not guarantee a hostname’s city placement. Mainland-bound offshore routes remain affected by border connectivity. Treat unverified endpoints as questions, not unconditional offshore/security/speed promises. See the cross-border offshore guide.

Origin placement and dynamic share shape performance

Assets can be served near buyers after hits, but login, inventory and payment commonly reach the origin. Edge delivery cannot remove every distance cost. Regional origins or edge logic may help, subject to state consistency, identity and data-location requirements. Keep personalized commerce responses out of shared caches.

Separate hot cache, cold fetch and dynamic trials, using P95, failures and business completion rather than average download speed. Normalize timing with the performance guide. Network maps select test locations; real-user evidence establishes post-rollout regional behavior.

Calculate prices by destination and component

At consultation, Cloudflare listed monthly Pro/Business at US$25/250, while CloudFront flat-rate Pro/Business were US$15/200 per distribution. Names, scope and allowances differ. Fastly separates delivery, requests and security; CDN5 bundles also require usage and incident terms.

Google Cloud CDN pricing listed first-tier Asia Pacific including Hong Kong at US$0.09/GiB and mainland destinations at US$0.20/GiB, served offshore. Destination rates do not prove mainland PoPs. Distinguish GB/GiB, fill and delivery, then add origins, logs, taxes and support.

Cross-border controls must support all clients

Payment providers, webhooks, SDKs and partners may operate in other countries and cannot complete interactive challenges. Separate policies by route and identity, authenticate signatures and validity, and retain auditable exceptions. Country-wide blocking can harm legitimate buyers. Verify crawler and proxy identity rather than trusting arbitrary headers.

Control access and retention for logs containing identities and IPs. Restrict origin ingress so old addresses cannot bypass protection. Acceptance should include login, checkout, callbacks and crawling, distinguishing network, policy and application failures.

Design pilots and fallback around business markets

Pilot noncritical resources before primary-market application traffic, retaining a protected alternative. Synchronize certificates, keys, exceptions, origin authentication and logs; a spare CNAME is insufficient. Two CDNs sharing an origin or critical DNS may fail together, so examine dependency correlation.

Recommendations can be specific: evaluate CDN5 for Chinese-language security support, CloudFront for AWS integration, Cloudflare for unified global web management, Fastly for programmability and Akamai for managed enterprise needs. Validate regional experience, correctness, support and total cost. Overseas protection is a recoverable service path, not merely a location.
