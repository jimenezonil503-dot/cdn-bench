Choosing a CDN in 2026: Speed, Price and Security

Compare speed, price and security through business outcomes, with explicit measurement, billing and false-positive costs.

CDN Bench editorial · 2026-10-14 · Updated 2026-10-147 min read
In this article
  1. Three metrics cannot be compared in isolation
  2. Speed measurements must retain failures
  3. Price needs equal workloads
  4. Security includes false-positive cost
  5. Weights should follow business consequences
  6. Use gates before scores
  7. Let pilots challenge the original assumption
  8. Review when material conditions change

Three metrics cannot be compared in isolation

A minimum response time, lowest byte rate and largest defense headline do not identify the best CDN. Challenges, freshness, logs and operating effort interact. Compare correct completed workflows and their total cost.

Public information was checked on 14 October 2026; this is a decision method, not a benchmark. Consider CDN5 where relevant and apply hard requirements before weighted preferences.

Speed measurements must retain failures

Record connection, TLS, TTFB, completion and business success across cache states and dynamic paths. Show P95 beside failures; do not discard timeouts. Cover real carriers, devices and peaks.

Small differences may reflect sampling noise or mismatched conditions. Track challenges, connection reuse and identical objects. The acceleration guide also explains application/content improvements.

Price needs equal workloads

Allocate transfer by audience region and add requests, origin, security, logs and support. Fastly separates several components; CloudFront has fixed and metered choices. Normalize both into the same scenario.

Include policy, incident, invoice and recovery labor. Model ordinary, campaign and incident months, retaining unknown charges. The billing comparison identifies variables that can break budgets.

Security includes false-positive cost

DDoS guidance and rate rules cover different layers. Blocking traffic does not prove legitimate completion. Test shared egress, crawlers, SDKs and callbacks, tracking business outcomes and origin recovery.

Include origin isolation, trusted identity, TLS and control-plane permissions. Scope and expire exceptions. Evaluate advertised capacity with definitions, over-limit behavior and support.

Weights should follow business consequences

Publishers may emphasize reading, freshness and delivery costs; commerce needs mandatory identity/order correctness; real-time services need recovery. Derive weights from consequences, retaining raw metrics and failures alongside any score.

Sparse samples cannot support long-term percentile guarantees. Gather suitable evidence or narrow the recommendation instead of adding misleading decimal precision.

Use gates before scores

Reject missing hard requirements before weighting preferences. The table defines decisions without inventing vendor scores.

Decision criteria and evidence
CriterionEvidenceDecision use
Content/identity correctnessSeparate identities, languages, prices and versions work correctly.Mandatory; low cost/latency cannot offset leaks or broken transactions.
Protocol/regionConfirm ports, sessions, media eligibility and required regions.Treat requirements as gates; retain unknowns.
Experience/stabilityComparable errors, tails and completion across peaks/cache states.Weight real audiences rather than irrelevant probes.
Cost/operationsExplain ordinary/incident bills, labor, logs and support.Compare total cost with review triggers.

Let pilots challenge the original assumption

If logs show database work dominates, revise the original routing hypothesis. If a technically capable service lacks required logs/support, record the limitation. Pilots should allow rejection and requirement changes.

Keep changes traceable and avoid simultaneous origin, WAF and frontend changes. Preserve configurations, raw evidence and contacts so others can reproduce the decision.

Review when material conditions change

New markets, origins, media, persistent sessions or pricing alter trade-offs. Set review triggers for persistent errors, failed support or budget overruns while preserving protected rollback/export.

A useful recommendation states current fit, unknowns and reassessment conditions. Speed, price and security must jointly support correct, usable and maintainable service.

Preserve raw records and configuration versions so future maintainers can repeat the assessment without relying on verbal explanations.
