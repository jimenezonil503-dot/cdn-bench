Offshore CDN Recommendations for 2026: Pricing, Routes and Features

Compare offshore delivery eligibility, public pricing, routing, cache, security and protocols without treating “no ICP filing” as a technical certification.

CDN Bench editorial · 2026-10-14 · Updated 2026-10-147 min read
In this article

  2. Match prices to products
  3. Mainland and global audiences need different evidence
  4. Four easily missed feature checks

Define offshore service scope

“No ICP filing” commonly describes offshore-node delivery, not mainland access without eligibility requirements. Business, content, data and service terms still apply. Reassess eligibility before adding mainland nodes.

Evaluate CDN5 and compare JQCDN for Hong Kong-oriented requirements. Suitability depends on actual nodes, protocols and terms. Public information was checked on 14 October 2026; no benchmark results are invented.

This shortlist includes regional-security bundles and global delivery products without equating their guarantees. Add requests, origin, logs and security as applicable. Validate free and paid tiers separately.

Mainland and global audiences need different evidence

Offshore node count does not ensure stable cross-border peak performance. Test carriers and cities, including errors, cold fetches and dynamic workflows. Hong Kong pinning can disadvantage Western audiences; choose scope from user distribution.

Cloudflare China Network has separate subscription/domain conditions. Ask every candidate for actual service regions; geolocation alone is not contractual evidence. See Hong Kong selection.

Four easily missed feature checks

Validate both TLS legs, identity-safe cache controls, request-level logs and explicit persistent-session limits. A checkbox does not prove operational usefulness.

Test callbacks, SDKs and APIs against challenges/rejections. Browser verification cannot be applied indiscriminately. Frontend CDN access does not protect every administrative port. Include these gates in trials; see WebSocket selection.

Confirm permitted content before activation

Downloads, media, software updates and ordinary pages have different usage requirements. Disclose object size, frequency and authorization, and verify permitted use, object limits and abnormal-traffic policy. A working trial is not proof of long-term eligibility.

Retain control of DNS, certificates and accounts, including export and rollback. Check existing managed-host proxies before stacking services with unclear ownership.

Use a common workload for cost comparisons

Read Cloudflare, CloudFront and Google pricing with aligned units, terms and feature scope. Annual equivalents require their payment conditions; GiB and GB differ.

Model ordinary, campaign and abnormal-request months using real logs or explicit assumptions. Ask about attacks, blocked requests, overages and logs. Choose predictability or elasticity according to workload and operating capacity.

Put correctness before speed during migration

Prepare certificates, origin authentication, cache policies and exceptions before a limited rollout. Verify identity isolation, orders, callbacks and updates with stable application settings.

Restrict origins afterward while preserving monitoring. DNS and established sessions do not disappear instantly. Review audience, cost, support and features at renewal. Offshore eligibility is only one part of long-term fit.

Start small where appropriate, but reassess when transactions, downloads or real-time workloads arrive. Confirm export and exit options early.
