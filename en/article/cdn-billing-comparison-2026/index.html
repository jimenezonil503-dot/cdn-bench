Pricing and procurement

CDN Price Comparison in 2026: Transfer and Bandwidth Cost Analysis

Normalize workloads across transfer, bandwidth, regions, fill and requests, using explicit budget assumptions and invoice reconciliation.

Normalize the denominator first

Per-GB rates, subscriptions and bandwidth bills cannot be ranked without a common workload and feature scope. Missing components can create an illusory low price.

Public material was checked on 14 October 2026. Refresh tiers, regions, taxes and commitments before procurement. The billing primer covers models; this guide maps equal work into them.

Define your own reference workload

Extract regions, requests, bytes, cache states and object classes from logs, or label forecasts explicitly. Do not let each provider choose a favorable traffic mix. Equal bytes can hide very different request costs.

Include origin location, egress, fill and logs. Request hit ratio cannot directly estimate byte offload when misses are larger objects. Keep both dimensions and release/refill peaks.

A hypothetical ledger shows the method

This hypothetical workload has APAC/Western delivery, dynamic requests and release fill. Apply actual quoted rates to each input and add fixed components; confirm whether absent line items are included elsewhere.

Read the less visible pricing lines

Fastly separates delivery, requests and security and has package restrictions. Google CDN separates delivery/fill/lookups. Alibaba billing explains its models.

CloudFront offers fixed and metered choices; Azure Front Door includes base fees. Align security, origin and support before deriving comparable totals.

Account for billing-period boundaries

Dashboards, invoices and logs may use rolling windows, fixed periods and different timezones. Align boundaries, currency, units and product scope, including credits, refunds and taxes, before investigating numerical precision.

Smooth and bursty workloads can favor different models

Use the same traffic time series to compare smooth and bursty billing. Percentile methods apply only when contracted, with understood sampling and missing-data rules.

Warming, purge frequency and cache retention affect fill and origin cost. Keep these variables adjustable. Do not cut essential security/logging merely to reduce an immediate invoice.

Stress the budget with abnormal traffic assumptions

Vary requests, large-object delivery and misses separately to expose request/security, transfer/bandwidth and origin risks. This is budget sensitivity analysis, not a reason to generate attacks. Clarify blocked traffic, scrubbing and cost-protection conditions.

Assign tiered alerts and actions such as source investigation, scoped limits or key repair. Never reduce bills by automatically exposing a bare origin. See the buying guide.

Use invoices to validate the model

Reconcile requests, bytes and charges in matching units/timezones. Investigate retries, errors, sampling, tiers and period boundaries. Preserve pricing, currency, tax and contract versions for recalculation.

At renewal, price the old reference workload separately from growth. A useful comparison explains controllable costs and unknown terms rather than promising a permanent cheapest provider.
