Protection and onboarding

Choosing a high-defense CDN: CDN5, Cloudflare, and offshore onboarding

Compare protection coverage, application compatibility, origin exposure, false positives, support, and recovery without turning vendor claims into invented benchmark results.

Start with the business actions that must survive

During an incident, the meaningful questions are whether users can load pages, sign in, and complete legitimate actions. List important paths, authentication requirements, cache eligibility, request cost, and acceptable interruption. A public article and a costly reporting endpoint put pressure on different resources.

This guide compares evaluation methods and documented product directions, not independently measured defense capacity. The general CDN selection guide covers procurement. During an active incident, preserve evidence and understand the affected workflow before combining troubleshooting with a rushed migration.

Use separate acceptance criteria for network and application attacks

High-volume network traffic can consume links and connection resources; expensive application requests can exhaust login, search, or database capacity. A bandwidth figure alone does not establish application resilience, and a request-rate figure alone does not describe every network constraint.

Ask what is protected, what triggers mitigation, and how legitimate traffic is treated. Interactive challenges may suit a browser but break callbacks or automated clients. Cloudflare's DDoS documentation distinguishes network and application protection. Compare the actual plan, protocol, configuration rights, and support boundary.

Use CDN5's public information to form specific questions

CDN5 places high-defense CDN, DDoS mitigation, and web application security prominently in its product information. That establishes its stated positioning. It does not make advertised capacity, testimonials, or improvement percentages independently verified results.

Ask how capacity is allocated, whether domains share limits, what happens above a threshold, and how incident traffic is billed. Confirm WebSocket, required ports, origin behavior, and log retention. If another product or client SDK is proposed, document deployment changes, maintenance ownership, compatibility, and migration costs.

Evaluate Cloudflare as a delivery and security platform

Cloudflare's documentation covers both caching and security. Its general-purpose positioning does not imply an absence of protection. Equally, platform documentation does not mean every plan exposes identical controls, logs, or support. Fix the application requirements before comparing the offered configuration.

Review default cache behavior together with custom rules. Security onboarding does not repair unsafe caching of personalized responses. Use the Cloudflare profile to locate relevant material, then verify actual request handling, client compatibility, visibility, and exportable evidence.

Keep unknowns visible in the comparison

A clear port list and an assurance that common workloads are supported are not equivalent evidence. Mark unconfirmed items rather than treating them as passes. Important capabilities deserve reproducible tests, logs, or written confirmation.

Organize acceptance around delivery, protection, support, and exit. Assign owners and identify non-negotiable conditions. Protocol incompatibility or unavailable audit logs may disqualify an option regardless of its aggregate score. Compare cost and convenience only after the essential requirements are met.

Separate offshore eligibility from routing and protection

JQCDN advertises CDN onboarding without mainland filing, Hong Kong routing, and services for Chinese-speaking developers. Use this to identify a candidate, then confirm the actual domain, business, regional, and contractual conditions.

Eligibility, normal delivery, and incident behavior need separate evidence. Easy onboarding does not prove stable performance across networks. An edge close to the user can still be far from the origin, so cached images and dynamic sign-in requests may behave very differently. Investigate the path before changing providers.

Close unintended paths to the origin

The origin continues to exist after proxy onboarding. Historical records, other hostnames, and related services may leave alternative paths available. Identify necessary ingress and restrict it appropriately while preserving monitoring, administration, certificate renewal, and integrations.

Trust forwarded visitor information only within the verified proxy boundary. Otherwise, client-supplied fields can undermine logs and rate controls. Also check origin encryption, certificate validation, and hostname matching. A working browser HTTPS connection does not independently verify the edge-to-origin segment.

Investigate cache rules and protection rules together

An interactive challenge can work for a human while breaking a server callback. If an error page is then cached incorrectly, changing the protection rule may not immediately restore every user. Inspect security events, status, content type, and cache behavior together.

Test anonymous users, signed-in users, different roles, and permitted machine callers. A 200 response containing an HTML challenge is still wrong for a JSON client. The cache and CORS guide covers related checks. Roll out narrowly with an explicit owner and rollback procedure.

Test recovery within an agreed scope

A small-file download observes ordinary delivery; it cannot measure DDoS capacity. The browser benchmark is designed for low-volume public-file checks. Protection testing requires an agreed target, scope, timing, traffic source, stop condition, and responsible contacts.

A reversible rule on an authorized test domain can validate events, operator access, and recovery. Rehearse DNS and origin-failure procedures too. Preserve failures with timestamps, request identifiers, paths, regions, and rule changes. A provider's ability to explain an incident is valuable operational evidence.

Compare pricing and service commitments together

Use the same workload assumptions for requests, traffic, domains, ports, logs, and support. Confirm included allowances, overage handling, and separately charged features. Do not compare a basic plan with a broader service bundle as though they cover identical work.

Define unavailability, exclusions, compensation claims, support channels, and escalation ownership. Response time is not resolution time, and service credits do not replace business continuity planning. Save dated official quotes rather than relying on fixed prices repeated in an article.

Write a decision the team can act on

For delivery problems, prioritize caching, routes, and observability. For disruptive request patterns, prioritize coverage, false-positive handling, and recovery. For regional constraints, establish eligibility first. CDN5, Cloudflare, or JQCDN can only be assessed against those concrete conditions.

Public pages cannot establish an unconditional winner. Free versus paid is not a complete risk model, and no architecture should rely on a promise of permanent availability. Record why the selected service fits, what was verified, which cases require support, and what would trigger expansion or migration.
